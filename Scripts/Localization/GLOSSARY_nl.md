# Dutch (nl): as Apple's Dutch iOS and Dutch-speaking Muslims write it

For Muslims in the Netherlands and Flanders (many of Moroccan, Turkish or Surinamese family, many born there,
many converts): standard Dutch in Apple's `nl` wording, no regionalisms. Address the reader with "je", lowercase
(je, jij, jouw; never "u"): "Tik op …", "Kies …", "Schakel … in". **A button, menu item or switch that starts
with a verb takes Apple's imperative, verb first: "Deel", "Voeg notitie toe", "Verberg Arabische tekst", "Sta
meldingen toe"; never the infinitive ("Notitie toevoegen": the brief's examples show the sentence case only).**
Titles are nouns ("Gebedstijden"). Sentence case, never English Title Case.

**Transliteration, one policy:** an Arabic term keeps the KEY's English-style spelling, never a Dutch
respelling: shirk, wudu, ghusl, sunnah, dua, dhikr, adhan, iqamah, tajweed, sukoon, ijazah, ishmam (not sjirk,
woedoe, soenna, tadjwied, soekoen): names, surahs and letter names must stay as the keys write them (rules 3 and
7), and two systems would drift. Where the keys spell a term several ways, use the spelling given here.
Translate what the English says: an Arabic term stays ("salah" salah, "makhraj" makhraj); an English word
becomes Dutch ("prayer" gebed, "ablution" kleine wassing). Only established Dutch words replace the key's:
Koran, soera, vers (ayah), profeet, moskee, imam, islam, moslim, Ramadan, hadj, djinn (jinn would read "yinn"),
jihad, Kaaba, Mekka, Medina, Suikerfeest, Offerfeest, Thora; places take their Dutch name (Jeruzalem, Teheran).
**Capitals:** Allah, de Koran, de Profeet and de Boodschapper (Muhammad ﷺ), and Hij / Hem / Zijn for Allah where
the key capitalises He / His. Lowercase, as Dutch spelling has them: de islam, islamitisch, moslim, profeet
Musa. Ramadan capitalised, as Apple's Dutch Hijri calendar writes it.

**Typography:** quotes “…” for a quoted label and every example word (“acht”, “think”), not Apple's ‘…’, which
the validator does not read as quotes. "&" becomes "en", as in Apple's "Privacy en beveiliging". No space before
? ! : ; …; the ellipsis is one glued character ("Meer…"). Percent glued, as Apple formats it: "50%" ("%lld%%");
units after a space ("5 km"); short units "min" and "u". Plural apostrophe ’s as in Apple's Foto’s: soera’s,
pagina’s, dua’s. ALL CAPS headers stay ALL CAPS; IJ is one letter (IJSLAND, "IJzer"). Compounds: one word, or a
hyphen, never two words: Dutch parts together ("gebedstijden", "Koraninstellingen"); a hyphen after a
transliterated term, a tab or a brand ("tajweed-kleuren", "Hijri-datum", "Fajr-gebed", "Hadith-instellingen",
"iCloud-back-up").
**Placeholders:** `%@` may be a de or a het word and takes no ending: no article, adjective or "elk / elke"
before it, no "%@'s". Put a noun or preposition you control in front, or a colon: "soera %@", "van %@", "in %@",
"door %@", "om %@" (a time), "%@ geleden", "Gebed: %@", "%@ wordt geladen…".
**Plurals** (brief): forms where the noun agrees: vers / verzen, soera / soera’s, pagina / pagina’s, dag /
dagen, minuut / minuten, hadith / hadiths, tel / tellen, dua / dua’s, rak'ah / rak'at, riwayah / riwayat.
Singular after any number, so a plain string: uur, keer (times, ×), jaar, procent, kilometer, juz, hizb. No
ordinals ("vers %lld").

## Tajweed and Arabic letters
unchanged: harakat (vowel signs: klinkertekens), fatha, kasra, damma, shaddah, tanween, madd (natural madd:
natuurlijke madd), ghunnah, qalqalah, idgham, iqlab, wasl · sukoon (for sukun too) · ikhfa (for Ikhfaa) ·
idh-har (for Idhhar, Idhaar, izhar) · tafkheem (for tafkhim; heavy: zwaar) · tarqeeq (light: licht) · waqf
(stopping: stoppen) · hamzat al-wasl (for Hamzatul WaSl and every spelling) · makhraj (pl. makharij) · sifat
(for sifaat; qualities: eigenschappen) · sun / moon letters zonneletters / maanletters · count (madd length) tel
("2 tellen").
**Articles:** every Arabic term and every letter name is "de" (de sukoon, de madd, de ghunnah, de tanween, de
makhraj, de hamzah, de riwayah; de alif, de noon, as Dutch says "de a"); Dutch nouns keep theirs (het vers, het
teken, het articulatiepunt, het partikel). Arabic adjectives take no ending ("een sakin letter").
**Letter names** stay exactly as the key writes them in that string, capitals included, in prose too: case
carries meaning (Haa ح, haa ه; Taa ط, taa ت; marbuuTah). The letter is hamzah; Hamzah is the Kufan reader.
**Rule names:** labels and titles in sentence case ("Madd lazim", "Idgham shafawi", "Idh-har shafawi", "Noon
sakinah"); in prose lowercase ("de madd lazim"), with "noon sakin" / "meem sakin" for every key spelling.
**Sound examples** (rule 4): exact in Dutch: خ the ch in “acht”; ي the j in “ja”; ش the sj in “sjaal”; long ii /
uu the ie in “zie” / the oe in “doe”, held long (the key's uu is never Dutch uu). Not exact, so keep the English
word, named as English: غ (the northern g is voiceless), و (Dutch w differs), ج, ث, ذ, ر, ح, ع, ق. A statement
about English stays about English.

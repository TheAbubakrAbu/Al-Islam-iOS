A: Shortening prayers is not the only option when you're traveling. The Prophet never prayed four rak'ah on a journey and neither did his Companions, so shortening is the Sunnah and the safer course [2]. But if you're staying in one place for a week, scholars generally say it's better not to combine prayers: pray each prayer in its time, shortened if you're still a traveler [2].

1. Pray each prayer in its usual time, shortened if you're still traveling [2].
2. Avoid combining prayers while you're away [2].

SUGGESTIONS: Give an example of nisab zakat from the Prophet’s life | What does the Quran say about nisab zakat? | What is the opposite of nisab zakat?
A: The nisab is the minimum amount of wealth a Muslim must possess before they are obligated to give zakat, which is the obligatory charity. The app and the Prophet's words clarify that this measure is tied to a person's own needs and dependents.

The Quran makes it clear that zakat is not based on a fixed sum but on a person's actual food and basic living costs. The words of Allah in Surah Ar-Rum [4] say that what you give in zakat out of desire for Allah's approval is what truly multiplies. The Prophet also clarified that this measure includes those who rely on you, including children born before sunset on the last day of Ramadan [2]. The Prophet said in a hadith that neither an old nor a defected animal, nor a male-goat may be taken as zakat unless the collector wishes it [6].

Scholars generally explain that the nisab is calculated from what you hold, including cash, savings, and investments, but personal items like your home or car are excluded [7]. The app's article notes that giving for the unborn is recommended, though not required [2]. The Prophet's words further establish that the act of giving, even a small amount, protects you from hardship [3].

A: The dua for Istikhara is:

“O Allah, You are the Sovereign, none has the right to be worshipped except You.” [4]

Its meaning in English is: “Indeed, prayer has been decreed upon the believers a decree of specified times.” [4]

A: Saving money is grounded in faith and practice, and scholars generally explain that it is tied to how one treats wealth and others.

1. The Prophet said that giving to dependents and those in need is a priority, and the best charity comes from surplus funds [6].
2. Wealthy people are encouraged to give from what remains after their own expenses, and withholding help from others can lead to divine provision [6].
3. A lesson from the Prophet’s companions shows that honesty in trade and responsibility in practice are central to belief, which includes managing resources responsibly [2].
4. The warning in Surah Al-Baqarah (2:177) links righteousness to giving to relatives, orphans, travelers, and the needy, showing that financial responsibility is part of a moral life [3].
5. The Prophet also emphasized that forgiving and helping those who have left their homes for the cause of Allah is a duty, even when personal ties make it hard [7].
6. Scholars note that cheating in trade and treating money as separate from prayer or community harm the soul, so prayer and livelihood are not to be divided [2].

Saving money in line with this teaching means balancing giving with personal needs, avoiding greed, and remembering that wealth is a trust. For guidance tailored to your situation, you might speak with someone knowledgeable about Islamic finance.

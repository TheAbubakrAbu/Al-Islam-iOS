A: The Quran presents Ayat al-Kursi as a declaration of divine sovereignty and the limits of human agency. Allah is described as the Ever-Living, the Sustainer of all existence, and nothing can intervene with Him except by His permission [1]. The verse also states that Allah knows everything that is present and everything that will come, and nothing exists beyond His will [1].

The Prophet is reported to have said that this verse is the greatest Ayah in the Book of Allah [3]. Another account mentions that when someone asked the Prophet about the greatest Ayah, he named Ayat al-Kursi [3]. These teachings highlight the extraordinary value and protection the verse holds for believers.

The Prophet is also recorded as saying that Allah's Speech is greater than the heavens and the earth [6]. This reflects the idea that the divine word itself surpasses all creation. The emphasis on this truth is tied to the broader theme of guidance found in the Surah [2].

These sources show that the verse is central to faith, serving as a source of merit and a reminder of divine authority. The Prophet's statements reinforce its special status within the Quran [1] [3].

A: The Quran speaks about patience in ways that center on trust and resilience. It says, “O you who have believed, seek help through patience and prayer. Indeed, Allah is with the patient” [1]. Elsewhere, it instructs, “And be patient, [O Muhammad], and your patience is not but through Allah. And do not grieve over them and do not be in distress over what they conspire” [2]. The Prophet’s words reinforce this message. A report from `Amr bin Taghlib, recorded by Imam al-Bukhari, notes that he gives to those who lack patience but leaves those who are patient and content [7]. These teachings show that patience is not just a personal virtue but a response shaped by faith and reliance on God.

A: Tawakkul is trusting in Allah while using lawful actions. The scholars say this trust is shown when someone takes steps in life but leans on Allah’s guidance above all else.

The Quran makes this clear: in Surah Al-A'raf, Allah says the faithful rely on Him and that His knowledge covers everything [3]. In Surah Ali 'Imran, the Prophet confirms that relying on Allah is a foundation for knowing right from wrong, even when signs appear [4]. These verses show that true reliance is not about giving up effort but about knowing that outcomes rest with the Most High.

The Prophet’s words reinforce this same idea. In a report from Jabir bin ‘Abdullah, he told a leper to eat with trust in Allah and reliance upon Him [5]. Another account from 'Adi b. Hatim explains that when hunting, one must use lawful means but not eat game if a dog has eaten it, because that would imply control over the hunt rather than trust in Allah [6]. The articles note that tawakkul means taking responsibility with the heart while leaving the rest to God [7] [8].

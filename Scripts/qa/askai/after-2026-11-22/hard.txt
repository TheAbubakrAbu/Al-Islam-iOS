A: The Quran and the hadith present Khidr as a wise companion whose presence carries a lesson in patience and restraint. The story begins when Moses asked Khidr to teach him sound judgment [1] [2]. Khidr agreed but set a condition: Musa was not to question anything distasteful until Khidr himself initiated the discussion [2].

The Prophet and companions later learned that Khidr had deliberately taken a boy, the finest and most handsome among others, and killed him [3] [4] [5]. This act was not random; it was tied to the boy’s destined path and the broader lesson Khidr imparted [3] [5]. Khidr also revealed the meaning of events that would otherwise elude patience [6].

The sources do not name Khidr’s origin, but they describe him as someone who enforces a boundary between guidance and harm, a lesson repeated through the journey and the story of the boy [2] [4]. Scholars note this account is consistent across traditions, though interpretations of the boy’s role vary [3] [5].

A: The nisab is the minimum amount of wealth that a Muslim must possess to be eligible to give zakat. According to the app, this includes cash, savings, gold, silver, money owed that is expected to be repaid, and business goods, but excludes personal items like a home or car [3].

The Quran clarifies that zakat is given to those in need, and the Prophet explained that zakat does not reduce wealth [1] [8]. The app's articles note that zakat is a form of charity that raises status and is linked to faith and humility, with the Prophet stating that saving for the afterlife even through small acts of giving is significant [2] [4].

In practice, the nisab is defined by the Prophet himself, who ruled that neither old nor defective animals, nor male goats, qualify unless the collector chooses to take them [5]. The practice is supported by reports that the Prophet would drink from a garden near the mosque, symbolizing generosity toward others [7].

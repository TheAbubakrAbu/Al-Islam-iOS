A: The verse you asked about is 1:4 from Surah Al-Fatihah (The Opener) [1]. It says, “Sovereign of the Day of Recompense” [1].

This sovereignty is about God’s authority on the Day of Judgment, when no one else will claim ownership of anything [1]. The tafsir explains that the word Malik here points to God’s control over the Day of Resurrection and the Day of Requital, showing that no one can speak without His permission [2].

How the readings (qiraat) differ:
1. Some read māliki yawmi d-dīni, meaning “Owner of the Day of Judgement,” which shows God’s control over the reckoning [1].
2. Others read maliki yawmi d-dīni, meaning “King of the Day of Judgement,” which describes God’s authority and sovereignty [1].

A: The Quran speaks of Ayat al-Kursi as a declaration of divine sovereignty and the limits of human agency. In Surah Al-Baqarah, Allah states: “Neither drowsiness overtakes Him nor sleep. To Him belongs whatever is in the heavens and whatever is on the earth. Who is it that can intercede with Him except by His permission?” [1]. This verse is presented as a foundation for recognizing that nothing exists beyond His will.

The Prophet is said to have described this verse as the greatest in the Book of Allah. In a report from Imam Ahmad, Ubayy bin Kab mentioned the verse when asked about the most meritorious ayah, and the Prophet affirmed its importance [3]. Another account from the same collection adds: “Allah has not created in the heavens nor in the earth what is more magnificent than Ayat Al-Kursi” [6].

These accounts highlight that the verse carries deep spiritual value and is central to the faith. The tafsir of Ibn Kathir notes that tremendous virtues are linked to it, reinforcing its role in guiding believers [3].

The Quran also emphasizes that the preservation of creation is beyond His need, reinforcing the idea that His knowledge is absolute [1]. This is reflected in the broader context of the surah, which addresses themes of guidance and divine wisdom [2].

A: The Quran and the hadith present Ayat al-Kursi as a central declaration of divine sovereignty and protection. In the Quran, Allah’s words in Surah Al-Baqarah [1] show that He is the Ever-Living, the Sustainer of all existence, and that no one can intercede with Him except by His permission. The same surah [2] frames this truth as part of guidance given to the Jewish people, urging them to accept the same truth revealed to Prophet Moses.

The hadith by 'Abdullah bin Mas'ud, recorded by Imam Tirmidhi [6], describes Ayat al-Kursi as the greatest speech of Allah, stating that no creation in the heavens or earth surpasses it. This reflects the idea that the verse holds a special, unmatched authority in the divine text.

Both the Quran and the hadith emphasize that Allah’s knowledge covers all that is present and all that will come, and that His dominion extends over everything [4] [7]. The verses and the narration together reinforce that this truth is not something created but a fundamental reality established by the Creator.

A: The Quran speaks of patience as a path to strength and trust in Allah. In Surah Al-Baqarah, Allah’s words in verse 2:153 say, “O you who have believed, seek help through patience and prayer. Indeed, Allah is with the patient,” [1]. Elsewhere, Surah An-Nahl, verse 16:127, add that patience is “through Allah,” and Surah Al-Ma'arij, verse 70:5, call for “gracious patience,” [2] [3]. These verses show patience as a steadfast quality that brings security and peace.

The Quran also frames patience as a response to hardship. In Surah Al-Kahf, verse 18:67, Allah’s words note, “He said, 'Indeed, with me you will never be able to have patience,’” [4]. This suggests that patience is a divine gift that cannot be forced. Together, these passages present patience not as weakness but as a reliable source of support.

The Quran further connects patience to humility and surrender. In Surah Al-Baqarah, verse 2:45, Allah’s words say, “it is difficult except for the humbly submissive [to Allah],” [6]. This shows that patience is rooted in recognizing one’s place before the Creator. These teachings form a clear call to carry patience with dignity and reliance on Allah.

A: Tawakkul is the act of trusting in Allah while still using lawful ways.

The Quran shows tawakkul when the believers rely on Allah even when there is no clear way out, as in the story of Ibrahim [3]. The same idea appears when Allah tells the faithful that everything they depend on is within His knowledge, and they must turn to Him [3].

The Prophet explained that tawakkul means acting with faith in Allah while taking proper steps, saying that relying on Him is the foundation of trust [7]. The articles note that tawakkul is about focusing on worship, gratitude, and self-control, and it strengthens community bonds [7] [8].

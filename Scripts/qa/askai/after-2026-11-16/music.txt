A: The evidence suggests music is not outright forbidden, but its use carries certain warnings.

1. The Prophet said, "All drinks that produce intoxication are Haram (forbidden to drink)" [8].
2. It was narrated that people among the Prophet's nation would drink wine, hear music, and singing girls, and Allah would cause the earth to swallow them up, turning them into monkeys and pigs [4].
3. Aisha reported that the Prophet said, "Musical instruments of Satan in the house of Allah's Messenger (ﷺ)" [5] and later said, "Leave them" [7].
4. The Companion Ibn Masud commented that the verse refers to singing [3].
5. The Prophet taught dhikr, but set formulas, counted litanies, swaying circles, music, and dancing are not from him [6].

Some sources warn against music when linked to intoxication or mockery of faith [2] [4]. A collection also notes that Companions questioned whether music was harmful, but the Prophet did not outright ban it [6]. Scholars differ on whether music is inherently forbidden: some see it permissible when not linked to intoxication, while others restrict it in certain contexts [6].

In Surah Al-Luqman, Allah says that some people buy amusement to lead others away from the path of God without understanding, and those people face a harsh punishment [2]. The Prophet also warned that musical instruments and singing can be used to mislead, pointing out that this warning specifically refers to singing [3].

In another account, the Prophet spoke about people who drink wine, use music, and have singing girls, warning that God will cause the earth to swallow them and turn them into animals [4]. Aisha recounted a moment when Abu Bakr protested that musical instruments in the Prophet’s home were linked to Satan, and the Prophet replied that this applies to every nation’s celebration [5].

These teachings show that certain forms of music and singing can be harmful if they distract from faith, but the texts do not forbid all music entirely. The Quran emphasizes that those who follow its guidance are the ones who truly believe [6]. The Prophet’s words suggest that music should not overshadow spiritual focus, especially during sacred times [7].

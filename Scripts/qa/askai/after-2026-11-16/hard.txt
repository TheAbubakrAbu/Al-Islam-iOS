A: The shortening of prayers while traveling is permitted, but certain exceptions are made.

1. The Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) never left behind the two rak'ah before Fajr and Witr [2].
2. A narrator from Salim bin 'Abdullah said he did not combine prayers except at Jam when his wife wrote to him in distress [3] [8].
3. The practice is tied to specific circumstances: the rawatib are dropped on a journey except those two, and voluntary prayers are still allowed [2].

Some scholars view leaving a prayer knowingly as serious, with differing views on making it up: Ibn Baz and Ibn Uthaymeen said it is disbelief unless the obligation is denied, while al-Albani said it is not unless the duty is openly rejected [5].

A: The nisab is the minimum amount of wealth that a Muslim must possess to become liable for giving zakat, the obligatory charity. The app explains that this includes cash and savings, gold and silver, money owed that is expected to be repaid, business goods, and investments held for profit, but excludes personal items like a home or car [3].

The Quran clarifies that zakat is not just about giving wealth but about giving with the desire for Allah’s approval, as the verse states that such giving “are the multipliers” [1]. The Prophet also clarified that zakat is not about diminishing wealth, saying charity does not reduce possessions and that humility for Allah’s sake raises status [2]. The hadith adds that zakat is tied to specific obligations, such as avoiding certain animals for the collection process [5].

The app’s articles note that zakat covers more than just financial needs, including aiding travelers and those in need, and that giving for the unborn is recommended but not required [6]. The Prophet himself practiced giving in ways that aligned with these broader principles, such as visiting a garden to drink water [7].

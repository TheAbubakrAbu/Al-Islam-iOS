A: The mahr is the marriage gift given by one partner to the other, meant to show respect and cover the costs of the union.

The Quran makes it clear that this gift is part of the lawful arrangement: Surah An-Nisa, verse 4:24 [4] says the mahr is an obligation, and Surah Al-Ahzab, verse 33:50 [3] lists several groups eligible to receive it. The Prophet also clarified its purpose in the hadith of Anas bin Malik, recorded by `Abdul `Aziz bin Suhaib in Sahih al-Bukhari 4201 [6]: he said the mahr was the captive woman herself, because manumission carried with it a value that served as the gift.

The practice is grounded in fairness, as the Quran's verse 4:20 [4] warns against returning a large mahr when replacing a wife, and the hadith of Abu Ajfa As-Sulami in Sunan Ibn Majah 1887 [5] shows that Umar bin Khattab believed the mahr should reflect dignity, not excess.
